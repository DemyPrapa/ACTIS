Adaptive Cooling and Temperature Intelligence System

Comfort that arrives before the heat does.

A.C.T.I.S. is an adaptive thermal management system for scoliosis braces and prosthetic sockets. It tracks skin temperature and humidity, predicts rising heat and cools ahead of discomfort.

The problem

Essential devices that trap heat and sweat.

Scoliosis braces and prosthetic limbs are essential, yet prolonged use can cause thermal discomfort. The impact differs between the two groups, but the cause is shared: a close-fitting device that holds heat and moisture against the skin.

18–20 h

Daily brace wear is often prescribed, which is why comfort matters for adherence.

27%

of 44 parents cited excessive sweating as a reason their children were reluctant to wear the brace (2025 study).

54%

average share of lower-limb prosthesis users who experience thermal-related problems (2025 scoping review).

Scoliosis braces

Brace treatment aims to stop the spinal curve from progressing during growth. Rigid, custom-made plastic braces enclose the upper torso, and their non-porous design limits heat loss and sweat evaporation. Heat and discomfort make the brace hard to wear, which can undermine adherence, compromise outcomes and raise the risk of curve progression, bringing patients closer to the threshold for surgery.

Prosthetic sockets

The socket fits around the residual limb, connects it to the prosthesis and transfers body weight and forces during movement. Heat, moisture, pressure and friction build up at this interface, causing discomfort and skin irritation, and can make the prosthesis harder to wear in everyday life.

The solution

One compact system for two applications.

A.C.T.I.S. is a compact, battery-powered thermal management system designed to be thin, lightweight, quiet and discreet. It adapts to both scoliosis braces and prosthetic sockets.

Sensing and control

Thin sensors track skin temperature and local humidity, and a compact microcontroller runs the system.

Thermoelectric cooling

Thermoelectric elements provide active cooling in a low-profile form.

Thermal-spreading layer

An internal layer distributes cooling across the whole skin-contact area.

Passive heat sink

A low-profile heat sink dissipates the heat drawn away from the skin.

Phase-change materials (PCM)

PCM work alongside the thermoelectric components to manage heat.

Breathable materials

Breathable materials further support moisture management.

How it works

A loop that learns the wearer.

  1. 01

    Sense

    Sensors record skin temperature and humidity for as long as the device is worn.

  2. 02

    Predict

    Machine learning models analyse the wearer's own temperature and humidity patterns and anticipate unfavourable changes.

  3. 03

    Cool

    Cooling starts before heat build-up causes discomfort and adjusts to the wearer's needs.

  4. 04

    Review

    A Bluetooth mobile app shows live readings and history, and the wearer can adjust cooling manually.

Instead of switching on at a fixed temperature, the controller learns each wearer's patterns. Users keep manual control.

Who it serves

For the people who wear the devices and the people who fit them.

A.C.T.I.S. follows a B2B2C model: it serves users directly and reaches them through orthotic and prosthetic partners.

People in brace treatment

Including parents and caregivers of adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis, who may purchase directly.

People using prosthetic limbs

Who deal with heat and perspiration inside the socket during daily activity.

Orthotic and prosthetic labs and manufacturers

Who design and produce braces and prostheses, and could integrate or offer A.C.T.I.S. alongside them.

Clinics and rehabilitation centres

Orthopaedic clinics, rehabilitation and physiotherapy centres, orthotists and prosthetists, as customers or distribution partners. Better comfort and adherence may also benefit their clinical practice.

What exists today

Moisture-wicking textiles, specialised undershirts, ventilated designs and phase-change materials may give some relief, but they do not adequately address heat and moisture build-up during prolonged wear.

Scoliosis braces No dedicated active-cooling solution currently exists.
Prosthetic sockets Active cooling has been explored in research prototypes, but commercially available solutions remain extremely limited.
